Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between 301 and 302 redirects?

301 Moved Permanently means the resource has moved to a new address permanently, and browsers and search engines will update bookmarks and indexes; 302 Found means a temporary jump and the original address is still in use. Both redirect automatically, but 301 passes SEO weight more explicitly. If you need to keep the original request method (e.g. POST stays POST), use 307 (temporary) or 308 (permanent).

What is the difference between 401 and 403?

401 Unauthorized means the request lacks valid authentication credentials and the server requires you to log in first or send a token; 403 Forbidden means you are authenticated but the current account has no permission to access the resource. In short: 401 asks who you are, while 403 says you do not have permission.

How should I handle 5xx errors?

5xx indicates a server-side error. Common cases: 500 (code exception, check the server logs), 502/504 (no response from or timeout on the gateway/proxy upstream; check whether the backend is alive or overloaded), 503 (service temporarily unavailable, often during maintenance or rate limiting; retry later per the Retry-After header). Most 5xx errors require server-side troubleshooting; clients can retry first to confirm whether it is intermittent.
