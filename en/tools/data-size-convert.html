Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between the 1024 and 1000 bases?

Computers work in powers of 2, so 1 KiB = 1024 B, while storage manufacturers and networks usually use the decimal system, where 1 KB = 1000 B. The same number converts differently under the two bases, which is why a hard drive's labeled capacity differs from the capacity shown on your computer. This tool lets you switch with one click.

Do KB and KiB mean the same thing?

Strictly speaking, no: KB is a decimal kilobyte (1000 B), while KiB is a binary kibibyte (1024 B). The two are often mixed up in everyday speech, but they should be distinguished in technical contexts. This tool uses KiB/MiB/GiB labels in the 1024 base.

Why does a hard drive labeled 1 TB show only about 931 GB on a computer?

Manufacturers label it as 1 TB = 1000 GB = 1,000,000,000,000 B, while the operating system calculates in the 1024 base: 1,000,000,000,000 / 1024 / 1024 / 1024 is about 931 GiB, displayed as roughly 931 GB. The values are consistent; only the base convention differs.
