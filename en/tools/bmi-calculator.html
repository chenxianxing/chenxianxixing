Frequently Asked Questions

What BMI is considered normal? What is the difference between the Chinese and WHO standards?

Chinese standard: under 18.5 underweight, 18.5-23.9 normal, 24-27.9 overweight, 28 and above obese. WHO standard: under 18.5 underweight, 18.5-24.9 normal, 25-29.9 overweight, 30 and above obese. Asian populations tend to carry more body fat at the same BMI, so the Chinese standard has a lower upper limit.

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE? How do I choose the activity factor?

BMR (basal metabolic rate) is the minimum energy needed to sustain life at rest, calculated here with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation. TDEE (total daily energy expenditure) = BMR × activity factor, reflecting total burn including daily activity and exercise. Bedridden is about 1.2, lightly active 1.375, moderately active 1.55, very active 1.725 and extremely active 1.9; pick the level closest to your weekly exercise frequency.

Is it safe to eat 500 kcal less per day for fat loss?

A 500 kcal deficit below TDEE corresponds to losing about 0.5 kg per week, a moderate and sustainable pace. For muscle gain, add 300 kcal above TDEE and pair it with resistance training. Individual results vary; pregnant women, teenagers and people with chronic conditions should adjust under professional guidance.
