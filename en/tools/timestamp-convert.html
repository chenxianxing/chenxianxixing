Frequently Asked Questions

How do I tell seconds from milliseconds in a timestamp?

A 10-digit Unix timestamp represents seconds (elapsed since 1970-01-01), while a 13-digit timestamp represents milliseconds. This tool automatically detects the length: 10 digits are parsed as seconds and 13 digits as milliseconds, and you can also switch units manually.

What is the difference between local time, ISO 8601 and UTC?

Local time is shown in your device's time zone; ISO 8601 usually includes a time zone offset (such as +08:00); UTC is Coordinated Universal Time (zero offset). All three describe the same moment, just in different formats.

Why does the same timestamp show different times in different time zones?

A timestamp itself is time zone independent and records an absolute moment. Converting it to a readable date-time requires a time zone, so Beijing (UTC+8) and London display different readable times even though the underlying timestamp is the same.
