Frequently Asked Questions

What parts make up a JWT?

A JWT (JSON Web Token) consists of three parts joined by dots: the first part is the Header (which declares the algorithm and type), the second is the Payload (which carries business and standard claims), and the third is the Signature (produced by signing the first two parts with a secret key). Decoding the first two parts lets you view the content, but the signature must be verified on the server with the secret key.

What are the time fields exp, iat and nbf?

They are all Unix timestamps in seconds: exp is the expiration time, iat is the issued-at time, and nbf means the token is not usable before this time (Not Before). This tool converts them into readable times and shows badges for exp, iat and nbf indicating whether the token has expired or taken effect.

Is it safe to paste a JWT into a web page?

All parsing in this tool is done locally in the browser and no token is ever uploaded to any server. However, you should still be careful about pasting tokens that contain sensitive information (such as identity or permissions) on untrusted third-party sites. Only parse tokens in trusted local tools.
