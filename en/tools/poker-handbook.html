Poker Handbook

An online poker quick-reference handbook: hand rankings with exact probabilities, the Texas Holdem table flow and position guide, a 60+ term bilingual glossary, and Omaha and Short Deck rule differences. All content loads locally and works offline.

Table Flow
1
Blinds
Before each hand, the Small Blind (SB) and Big Blind (BB) post forced bets at the agreed amounts, forming the initial pot. Blinds usually increase one level every 15 to 30 minutes.
2
Pre-flop
Each player is dealt 2 hole cards. Action starts with UTG, to the left of the Big Blind, and each player may fold, call, or raise until no one raises again.
3
Flop
Three community cards are dealt, followed by a betting round. On every street after the flop (Flop/Turn/River), action starts with the SB, to the left of the Button (BTN).
4
Turn
The 4th community card is dealt. The board structure is largely set, another betting round follows, and the pot usually grows noticeably on this street.
5
River
The final community card is dealt, making 5 in total, and the last betting round takes place. No more cards are coming, and the final battle of value bets and bluffs unfolds here.
6
Showdown
If two or more players remain after the betting, cards are shown: each player freely combines 2 hole cards with the 5 community cards to make the best 5-card hand, and the strongest hand wins the pot.
Positions Overview
Texas Holdem seating (6-max)
Action moves clockwise
UTGUTG HJHJ COCO BTNBTN SBSB BBBB
  • UTG (Under the Gun): acts first before the flop; the worst position, so you need a very strong hand to get involved.
  • HJ (Hijack): a mid-early position between UTG and CO where you can widen your opening range appropriately.
  • CO (Cutoff): a strong position just left of the Button and a popular spot for stealing the blinds.
  • BTN (Button): acts last after the flop with the most complete information, making it the most profitable seat at the table.
  • SB (Small Blind): second to last to act before the flop and first to act after it, the worst position throughout the hand.
  • BB (Big Blind): has posted the larger blind, acts last before the flop, and may see the flop for free if no one raises.
Betting Rounds
  • Limit or No-Limit per game: in Limit the bet size on each street is fixed; in No-Limit you can push your entire stack in at once (All-in).
  • Action goes around the table once per street until all remaining players have matched the same bet, at which point the street ends.
  • If a player goes All-in, a main pot is set aside and the remaining players keep betting the excess into a side pot.
Minimum Raise Rule
A raise must be at least as large as the previous bet or raise. For example, with blinds of 10/20, the first raise is at least to 40; if someone raises to 100, the next minimum raise increment is 80 (to at least 180). If a player goes All-in for less than the minimum raise, the raise window does not reopen and others may only call or fold.
Side Pots
When a player goes All-in against opponents with more chips, the All-in player can only win a main pot matching their stack; the excess forms side pots among the remaining players. Multiple All-ins can create several side pots, which are settled one by one by hand strength.

Omaha

  • Each player is dealt 4 hole cards, and there are still 5 community cards.
  • Hand-building rule: you must use exactly 2 hole cards plus 3 community cards, no more and no fewer.
  • For example, with A-spades A-hearts K-diamonds Q-clubs, an A-diamonds on the board does not make three of a kind, because using only 1 hole card is illegal.
  • Because the number of hole cards you may use is restricted, hand strength is usually more concentrated than in Texas Holdem, and flushes and straights appear more often and bigger.
  • Common variants: Pot-Limit Omaha (PLO) and Omaha Hi-Lo, where the pot is split between high and low.

Short Deck (6+)

  • Remove the 2, 3, 4 and 5 and only 36 cards remain, which noticeably changes the distribution of hands.
  • The Ace can count as 5: the lowest straight is A-6-7-8-9, while 10-J-Q-K-A remains the highest straight.
  • A straight beats a flush, the biggest difference from standard rules.
  • Three of a kind (Set) beats a straight, and full houses are relatively more common.
  • High card and one pair appear less often, so outcomes depend more on big hands and draws.
The basic flow of both variants (blinds, four betting rounds, showdown comparison) matches Texas Holdem; the main differences are the number of hole cards, hand-building rules, and hand ranking order.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the order of poker hand rankings?

From strongest to weakest: straight flush (royal flush is the largest), four of a kind, full house, flush, straight, three of a kind, two pair, one pair, and high card. Straight flushes total only 40 combinations, about 0.0015%, while high card is the most common at roughly 50%.

What is the difference between Texas Holdem and Omaha?

In Texas Holdem each player has 2 hole cards and may freely use any 5 of the hole and community cards to make the best hand. In Omaha each player has 4 hole cards and must use exactly 2 of them plus 3 community cards, so the structure of hand strength and strategy differ greatly.

Which is bigger, a straight or a flush?

Under standard rules a flush beats a straight, but in Short Deck (remove 2-5) the rule changes so a straight beats a flush, the Ace can count as 5 for the lowest straight, and three of a kind (Set) also beats a straight.
